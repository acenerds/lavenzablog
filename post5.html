It's a wonder what being taken off of antidepressants will do to a person. I don't want this to be nothing but whining. I HATE people like that, forgive me, because I know that they are struggling, but jeez I just feel so infuriated and maybe even disgusted with their very existence. You know the type of talking about, the kind of people who go beyond simple annoying "oh boo hoo woe is me" and instead take every little thing as confermation of what they've always thought. It's hard to explain, so I guess I'll talk about myelf rather than others so I don't feel bad about being mean to mentally ill people.

When I get really sad and depressed it is exceptionally annoying because every little thing that occurs suddenly becomes more evidence that I in particular am the heaven's chosen target for suffering and misery. This type of mindset is gross because it makes me such a miserable downer to be around that other peole don't want to interact with me. This, in turn only solidifies my loneliness which means it adds to my belief that i am the one who suffers and was marked to never have anything good in their entire life, which only makes me more unlikeable and annoying, creating a toxic cycle and self fulfilling prophecy.

A similair experience happens to Asa Mitaka, my glorious beautiful perfect darling baby queen goddess mother, in which every failed social interaction or relationship is further proof that she is unlovable which then exasperates her social struggles and so on as the cycle continues. The difference is she's insanely cute so I'd never leave her or think she's unlikeable or unlovable or anything like that. I need her so bad !!

I think I wasn't really aware of just how much my antidepressants were helping and working until they're gone now. See, I kept making the joke of "Do these pills really work or did switching from Engineering to English just make things better" and unforutantely, the answer is "the pills obviously work stupid!" And now I regret it. For a time, things were really really really nice so I'm gonna compare things I guess.

With no meds I had a very strong reaction to seeing people happier than me. It's ugly, so so very ugly, but I had such a burning jealousy welling inside my heart when I saw peole holding hands or friendgroups laughing together. With pills, I was certainly still jealous when I saw a happy couple but now it was the kind of jealousy where I roll my eyes and gag and then move on instead of this sickening pit in my stomach knowing that no matter how hard I claw and bite and straggle that life I want it will always slip away from me.

With no meds I hated going to the gym, it was the usual type of thing where I'd go for like a week, maybe two, and then stop going because I'd lose motivation. With pills it was actualy very easy, I wouldn't go so far as to say I "enjoyed" it, I think people who claim they LIKE working out are insane and also lying and trying to convince themselves, but I certainly wasn't miserable. Now that misery has come back almost enfold. I am yet to go to the gym without either crying while working out or crying once I'm done working out. I hate it. It's all this hurt and this pain and this suffering and for what? What do I get out of it? I know supposedly if I stick to it, way down the line there will be a reward assuming I eat less too, but who cares? Yes I'm lazy, yes I want my reward now, yes I'm only working out in the hopes of looking good enough to not be alone anymore, is that so wrong?

Even sleep is affected, which is probably the most frustrating part. Maybe this one is just placebo though. Before, when I didn't have my pills I could hardly sleep as I would be overwhelmed by stress and fear and misery. In my head, my mind would be swimming with thoughts, ugly, boring, lame "oh boo hoo woe is me" thoughts. After the meds, I slept nicely actually. An now without them, I had a nightmare just last night that resulted in me waking up and coughing and vomitting and crying.

I don't actually remember the nightmare too much, but I think it had something to do with a poem I had been working with recently. I don't want to go into too much detail because I plan on writing about that poem too in an "On..." segment but essentially the author uses both the idea of being ripped apart from the inside and the idea of standing outside your body as a metaphor for love. In my nightmare, from what I can remember, there was a creature that held my body tightly in it's clawed hands and slowly peeled my skin from me, sort of like a banana. The experience was already painful enough but to add to that, I could also see all of it happen from a third person perspective. It was like I was both being torn and being forced to watch someone get torn, both physical and psychological torment. I don't remember the rest but I know my parents were there too whcih is sad to think about.
