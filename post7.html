On... Socialization

4/28/26

In today's On... piece, I will let out all of my incel rage. Okay obviously I'm not an incel but I think deep within my soul I have a bitter old codger and I have to let him out every so often cuz keeping him bottled up will result in an internal combustion and I'll die. It's like drinking a little bit of poison over a large course of time to develop an imunity vs jugging a whole bottle all at once. So let's all prepare to take a tiny shot of poison.

I'm scared of everything and everyone always and forever. I'm sure this is obvious to anyone who's ever interacted with me. It's all scary. Making eye contact, walking around, saying "please" and "thank you" it's all comparable to the horrors of war. I'm a veteran too, I survived talking to a pretty girl at a party one time. You wouldn't last a day out there where I served. Even talking to people on the internet is scary (hence why I might not always reply to your comment, sorry).

To live life with the instincts of a prey animal, especially the really pathetic ones who's hearts explode when they get too frightened (how could that POSSIBLY help evolutionarily, how did that trait even get passed down???), is actually not that bad. I just don't leave the house, don't make eye contact, and don't talk to people. That's fine for a prey animal but unfortunately (until further notice) I'm a human being. As the famous saying goes "No man is an island, entire of itself: but it'd be really really cool if we all were and we didn't need social interactions at all, right?" I mean, obviously I wanna improve and to improve is more than just the turtle hermit way of working hard, studying well, and eating and sleeping plenty. You gotta go outside and talk to people.

But guess what they don't tell you, oh student of the turtle hermit way? NO ONE WANTS TO TALK TO YOU!!! Everyone is evil and hates me and my prey animal instincts were right on the money!! I say "good morning" to someone in the hallway? Nothing. I say "Man, can you believe we have a test in this class already" to a classmate? Not even a peep. I say "How's it going?" to someone who walked into the kitchen. You'll never guess what they say. NOTHING!!! Not a damn thing! Even Satan has his companions, fellow-devils, to admire and encourage him; but I am solitary and detested. Oh woe is me! Boo hoo hoo! I am truly the worlds greatest victim.

And look, I get it okay, I really do! No one owes me nothing never. I know that! I know it better than anyone else, why do you think I'm so scared of talking to people in the first place? It's cuz I know "This person owes me nothing and they hate me and I'm bothering them just by breathing the same oxygen as them and being on the same planet as them." Okay? I'm an expert on this subject. When I say "good morning" and they don't say anything back, there's a MILLION reasons why they might not have. 1. They didn't hear me. 2. They hate me and want me to die. 3. They are in a rush. 4. Their parents just died in a hot air balloon accident and therefore they aren't in the mood to talk. I don't know! But that's the point. I. Don't. Know. I'm not supposed to know. I'm not asking to know. I'm asking for "good morning" and that's it. We are extras! We're not doing monologues. I'm not asking for a soliloquy I'm asking for TWO WORDS.

And beyond that fact. Even if I WERE trying to start a full conversation when you can't talk, you can just say that. It's so easy to say "Oh sorry I'm in a rush, gotta get to class" or "Phone call, sorry" or a million other things. But, okay, I hear what you're saying. "Lavenza what if I'm not in a rush and I don't have to take a phone call and I'm not in a bad mood I just don't wanna talk to you?" Well guess what? That's fine! That's awesome. I love that we live in a society where we can just choose to and not to talk to someone for no real reason, you realize how lucky that is? There's a fine and dandy solution. It's called LYING!! You don't actually have to be in a rush or on the phone, you can lie to my face and I will be forced to believe you! I say "Hey" and you can say "Sorry, I don't want to talk to you because I hate you and want you to die," OR you can say "Oh hey, gotta get to class, talk to you later" without actually needing to go to class or talk to me later.

And there you go again, "But Lavenza, you said that you understand that people don't owe you anything, yet now you're demanding that people lie to you or give you reasons. Shouldn't they not have to do that if they truly don't owe you anything?" Correct! That's cuz I lied. Or rather, there's layers to it that we ignore. Two things can be true at the same time. I think the Buddha invented that. Truth numero uno: No one owes anyone else their time, energy, or emotion, especially if it comes at a cost. Truth Numero deux: You actually do kinda owe people stuff just a little bit. Are those contradictory? Maybe. But just hear me out.

I find it so frustrating when I see someone complain that none of their classmates talk to them and someone inevitably responds, "Well, they're here to learn, not talk." Because THAT'S NOT TRUE. Or rather, sure, maybe that's what they're here for. Maybe you enrolled in college with the sincere intention of learning and absolutely no interest whatsoever in talking to another human being. Fine! I can't tell you what your goals are. But unfortunately, your goals do not alter the laws of reality. When you leave your house, you are technically accepting the possibility that a meteor might fall out of the sky and kill you. You don't want a meteor to hit you. You haven't consented to being meteor'd. You don't wake up every morning thinking, "God, I hope today's the day I finally get flattened by a meteor." But the possibility exists. You have left the house, you are under the sky, and therefore a meteor hitting you is now within the realm of objective possibility. That's just how the universe works. If a meteor does hit you, I think you're completely entitled to be upset about it, but you can't exactly say, "What the hell? I specifically came outside to go to the grocery store! I didn't agree to meteors!"

College is the same way. Maybe you didn't agree to have conversations. Maybe you didn't sign a contract saying, "I hereby consent to random classmates asking me how my weekend was." You don't have to want anyone to talk to you. You can hate small talk with every fiber of your being. But when you decide to spend four years living in a building with hundreds of other human beings and attending classes with hundreds more, you have entered an environment where someone might look at you and say, "Hey, what's up?" That's not an attack. It's not some bizarre violation of your personal autonomy. It's just one of the things that can objectively happen when you put yourself in a place containing other people. You wanna live in a world where no one will ever try to talk to you? I hear the Mariana Trench is quiet this time of year. That's about your only shot. Where there are humans, there will be human interaction!!

And obviously, none of this means you have to talk back. The fact that someone might talk to you doesn't mean you're obligated to give them your time, energy, or attention. If you're busy, tired, upset, wearing headphones, or simply don't feel like talking, that's completely fine. I'm not trying to establish some new constitutional amendment guaranteeing everyone the right to small talk. I'm saying that there is a baseline level of social participation that comes with living around other people. The rules don't say, "You must talk to everyone, be energetic and extroverted at all times, and give every interaction 100% of your attention." They do, however, say "Generally try to be polite and respectful to people who treat you politely and respectfully, including participating in the very short and simple exchange of greetings when you can."

I didn't write the rules, I think it was Rousseau who did, or something idk I didn't actually read that book. But the simple fact is, one can't live in a society, reap the rewards and benefits from living in that society, and also completely withdraw and shut themself off from society. It's like the opposite of "you can't have your cake and eat it too." It's my new version "You can't throw the cake away and then also be upset when you don't get to eat cake." In this case "cake" is social interaction. And again, you just might not be up for it all the time and that's fine. But you have to put a little bit of effort in. The easiest way to a clean and happy society is... empathy. Cringe, I know. I'm really on the soapbox right now. But rather than "put yourself in their shoes" I'm instead proposing "Put everyone in your shoes" which is to say, ask yourself the question "What if everyone did that?" When you don't say good morning back to someone ask, "Hey what if everyone did that?" and realize the answer is "Society would be miserably lonely." When you reject a tiny and innocent small talk chat about assignments or something dumb ask "What if everyone did that?" and realize no one would ever make friends ever.

The worst thing in the world is this fact: You are society! It's not "I'm me and everyone else is society," you are society. What you do, how you behave, how you carry yourself, is how other's are doing it all too. So always ask yourself "Would society still function if everyone acted the way I do?" I mean, that's why I've been forcing myself to go out in the first place, society would collapse if everyone was Lavenza! That sounds terrible! There shouldn't even be ONE Lavenza. So you gotta be Not-Lavenza and I'll do my best to be Not-Lavenza, and us two Non-Lavenzaians can do what good society-dwellers do, talk to one another.
