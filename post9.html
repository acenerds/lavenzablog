Laughing it off

9/28/26

Now, this may come as a shock to many of you considering how much I LOVE complaining on here but I'm generally a pretty positive person. More often than not, when bad things come my way I already know fighting is pointless so I just get over it.

Is this the healthiest philosophy in the world? Probably not, it's really not so functionally different from "Boys don't cry" or just bottling up all your feelings, but it's shockingly effective. At my core I do my best to find things that suck to be funny. My classmate ignored me after I waved at them? That's silly, what a dumb thing to do! I made a fool of myself in front of a cute girl, man how embarassing lol!

Just the other night I was talking to this utterly gorgeous girl at a party (yes the only thing I post about at this point is girls, who GAF this is my internet and I'm just kind enough to let you use it) and one of her friends just seemed to utterly hate me. But that became a funny story, not something that upset me. She first started insulting me for being "performative" because I was wearing a Laufey shirt and carrying a totebag. It seems utterly pointless to get upset at that so I just laughed and pulled out my copy of Crime and Punishment and said "I'm way worse than you think" while laughing.

Then she mentioned another girl that I know and I perked up and said "Oh I totally know her!" and for some reason she snapped "You you don't!" This was such an aggressive and unnecessarily rude act that I couldn't help but laugh again and say "You could be right, she's got a common name." (For reference, she does NOT have a common name).

She did more but I don't wanna detail everything, nor do I wanna make myself sound like I'm some super sexy and snarky detective who always knows the right thing to say. The point is just that I could have let that bother me, but it just felt too silly to really give any weight to. I mean, she was drunk and she seemed to have her mind made up about me, so it's not like I could have magically convinced her through charm and my endearing heart. And that's sort of what I'm getting at.

When there's nothing you can do, you should at least try to laugh about it. And if you can't laugh at yourself, can you really laugh at anything? So when things go wrong, and specifically wrong in a way that's totally out of my hands, what more can I do besides shrug my shoulders and laugh? There's some proverb about how it's like trying to grasp a river or the breeze, I think it's Buddhist.

Now, as I mentioned before, this maybe isn't the healthiest process. It's fun in the instant to be able to just laugh and move on, but in the long run things sort of come back to bite you. The other night I was drunk and I tripped, skinning my kneew. I have pretty low pain tolerance but normally wouldn't start sobbing violently over a skinned knee or anything, just a few whimpers and some dramatic limping at most. But for some reason, that moment felt like the end of the world. Obviously, it's because I was drunk. But in that moment, laying on my back on the cold concrete with the black empty sky crushing me from above, I couldn't help but cry. People were walking by, they were all kind of blurred into one big mass of colors and noises, and it only made me feel worse. They were together, they were happy, and I had a throbbing knee and a much lower ceiling than everyone else and no one bending down to help me up.

I couldn't really bring myself to laugh it off, even though a skinned knee is such a trivial issue!
