Laughing at Yourself

9/28/26

Now, this may come as a shock to many of you considering how much I LOVE complaining on here but I'm generally a pretty positive person. More often than not, when bad things come my way I already know fighting is pointless so I just laugh and get over it.

Just the other night I was talking to this utterly gorgeous girl at a party (yes the only thing I post about at this point is girls, who GAF this is my internet and I'm just kind enough to let you use it) and one of her friends just seemed to utterly hate me. She first started insulting me for being "performative" because I was wearing a Laufey shirt and carrying a totebag. Wanting to add fuel to the fire I pulled out my copy of Crime and Punishment and said "I'm way worse than you think" while laughing.

Then she mentioned another girl that I know and I perked up and said "Oh I totally know her!" and for some reason she snapped "You you don't!" Once again, there's nothing to be done in a situation like that.

She did more but I don't wanna detail everything, nor do I wanna make myself sound like I'm some super sexy and snarky detective who always knows the right thing to say. The point is just that I could have let that bother me, but it just felt too silly to really give any weight to. I mean, she was drunk and she seemed to have her mind made up about me, so it's not like I could have magically convinced her through charm and my endearing wit.

I try to avoid letting myself get stressed about situations where there's nothing I could do anyways. If it's out of my hands, it's not my responsibility. I can't be held accountable for natural disasters so why should I worry take accountability for stumbling over my words in front of a girl? I think if you can't laugh at yourself, you can't laugh at all. Every situation that could be bad gets turned into a funny story I can tell later. "Aw man you should've seen how lame I looked! Haha!"

Now, maybe this isn't the healthiest way of dealing with things. It's easy enough in the moment but I'm sure it'll come back to bite me eventually. Laughing at something doesn't necessarily mean you're over it. The other night I skinned my knee. I was drunk and wearing shoes that make me look taller and tripped. I've always had low pain tolerance but this was trivial, I'd limp around, maybe whimper for sympathy, and laugh about it the next day. Instead I found myself lying on the cold concrete, back to earth and eyes to the sky. It was big, black, and empty. Not a star to be seen. People walked by, their faces and bodies blurring all together into some kaleidoscopic mess of colors and noises. Everyone else seemed to be having such a good time. I had a throbbing knee and an extremely low ceiling. No one would kneel down to help me.

Even while drunk I knew that me crying there was just so ridiculous. There I was sobbing on my back while everyone else got to live their life normally. It was dumb and silly and yet I couldn't laugh. It's a lot harder to laugh at yourself when the joke has been on you for years. It's much easier to laugh when you're standing.
